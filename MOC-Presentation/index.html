Problem
Why we need this

The cost of manual change control

  • Paper forms get lost. A signed Annexure-H stuck in someone's tray is a stalled MOC — and a safety risk.
  • Approval chains break. When a manager is on leave, work waits days. There's no formal way to delegate.
  • No visibility. Leadership cannot see how many MOCs are open, where they're stuck, or which are overdue.
  • Audit trails are fragile. Who approved what, when, and why — that history lives in scanned PDFs and emails.
  • Temporary changes expire silently. Without alerts, a 90-day temporary MOC can outlive its window unnoticed.
⚠

Real consequences

Process safety incidents are tied directly to poor change control. The OSHA Process Safety Management standard explicitly cites "management of change" as a leading indicator of plant safety.

✓

What we're aiming for

100% traceability. Zero lost forms. Live KPIs. Automatic delegation. Audit-grade history at the click of a button.

Lifecycle
Stage by stage

The 4-stage MOC lifecycle

STAGE 1
Initiate
JRE files Change Request. Risk screening (Annexure-C) + classification (D1/D2). Field In Charge signs.
STAGE 2
Classify & Approve
Hierarchy chain reviews work-pack. Functional review by Mgr Production / MAI / HSE, then MPO/EM, then Dir Ops or Head EDP.
STAGE 3
Execute
Work pack executed under PTW. PSSR (Pre-Startup Safety Review) gate before re-commissioning.
STAGE 4
Close / PSSR
Drawings updated, dossier compiled, temporary changes reverted. Closeout signed off.
⏱

Temporary changes

Maximum 180 days. The system enforces an expiry date at creation, alerts the originator at T-30 days, and auto-flags overdue MOCs in red on every dashboard.

📌

Capital projects

If "Capital Project" is ticked on a Major MOC, Head EDP is added to the chain after Director Operations. Non-capital Major routes Head EDP earlier — both per §3.4.3.

Delegation
"What if my approver is on leave?"

Bumble-style delegation, but for safety.

Each user has a "Reports To" link. When approval lands in your inbox and you're unavailable, click Delegate and pick anyone in your reporting tree.

  • Transitive. Subordinates of subordinates count too. Your full reporting tree is eligible.
  • Audited. The original assignee, the delegate, and the timestamp are all preserved on the step record.
  • Indicated. The chain row shows a "delegated" badge so reviewers can see the rerouting.
  • Bounded. You can only delegate downwards. No accidental sideways or upwards transfer.

Status: Revision Required
①
Comments are mandatory

The system blocks a rejection without comments. The originator must know why.

②
Later steps cancelled

All approval steps after the rejection are marked "cancelled" — they don't show as pending.

③
Originator gets the ball back

The MOC re-opens for editing. Title, narratives, classification — all editable. Then resubmit.

On resubmit the chain is rebuilt from scratch. If the JRE upgraded the classification from Minor to Major, the new chain reflects that — automatically.

Stage Forms
Annexure C, D1, D2 — and beyond

Four structured forms across the lifecycle

🛡
Risk Screening

Stage 1 — JRE answers 8 yes/no/N-A questions covering SIL equipment, design envelope, emergency systems, P&ID changes, hazardous chemicals, regulatory permits, plant availability, training. Recommends classification + risk level.

📋
Internal Safety Review

Stage 2 — HAZOP, JSA, redlines, relief review, electrical area class, material MOC, training, spares. Outstanding actions captured.

✅
PSSR

Stage 3 — Pre-Startup Safety Review. Conducted on-site, walkdown findings, attendees, Cat-A actions closed, approved-for-startup gate.

🏁
Closeout

Stage 4 — Drawings updated, procedures revised, Cat-B actions closed, dossier compiled, temporaries reverted, verification recorded.

Every form supports Draft → Submit → Approve → Reopen. Submitter and approver are always different people — segregation of duty enforced at the API.

How we keep the system safe

JWT authenticationStateless tokens, configurable expiry, signed with a 96-character secret. Tokens stored in client localStorage with secure flag.

Bcrypt password hashingSalted, cost-factor 10. Plain passwords never written to logs or DB.

Anticipated Questions
Things you may want to ask

Common questions, answered

What if our procedure changes?

Position codes and chain definitions are configuration, not core code. A revision can be applied without downtime — usually within a day.

How do auditors get evidence?

The Audit Report PDF (date-range filterable) and the per-MOC Minute Sheet provide a complete record. Both are signed by the system clock and tamper-evident.

What if the network goes down?

Reads are served from the database. Cached UI assets work offline. Approvers can resume the moment connectivity returns. Unsubmitted drafts never lose data.

Can two approvers act in parallel?

Sequential by design — that's MSP-HSE-08. The system enforces order strictly. Parallel review is a deliberate non-feature.

Who owns the data?

Mari Energies. The DB lives on Mari-controlled infrastructure. The full schema and dump can be exported any time. No SaaS lock-in.

How fast to onboard a new approver?

About 90 seconds: Admin → Add User → set Department, Position, Reports-To. They get their login by email.
